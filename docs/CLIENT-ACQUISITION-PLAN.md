Prepared September 21, 2026. This is a starting offer and validation plan, not a claim that any price maximizes revenue.

## Positioning

Help established local service businesses turn interest into qualified inquiries with a clear website and focused Google Search campaigns. Lead with specific work prospects can inspect. Be transparent that Baryames is a family-business engagement. Do not imply proven revenue increases, fabricated testimonials, or an agency team. AI helps production; Chuck remains responsible for judgment, client communication, and quality.

Start with Greater Lansing businesses because local familiarity is a practical advantage. Choose buyers with capacity, a service worth promoting, clear customer economics, and someone responsible for prompt follow-up. Reject engagements that require a guaranteed return, unlimited work, or tracking the client will not help maintain.

## Capacity and profit

Start with three recurring clients, then aim for five to eight only after the service is stable. Do not discount in anticipation of handling 20 clients with AI.

Illustrative planning assumptions, not measured workload: 4 to 6 hours per recurring client each month after setup; 15 to 25 hours per onboarding; $50 per recurring client per month in allocated operating cost; another 20 to 30 hours monthly for sales and business administration, plus existing Baryames work. Track actual time from the first engagement.

| Active retainers | Monthly service revenue | After illustrative $50/client operating cost | Monthly delivery hours |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | $2,250 | $2,100 | 12 to 18 |
| 5 | $3,750 | $3,500 | 20 to 30 |
| 8 | $6,000 | $5,600 | 32 to 48 |
| 20 | $15,000 | $14,000 | 80 to 120 |

The third column excludes taxes, the value of Chuck's labor, additional overhead, onboarding, churn, and unpaid sales effort. It is not take-home profit. At 20 clients, sales/admin plus delivery could already consume 100 to 150 hours a month before onboarding or family-business work. AI does not remove accountability, client decisions, or support.

Track cash contribution = collected fees minus attributable costs. Also track economic profit = cash contribution minus total owner hours multiplied by a chosen hourly value. Measure sales time, revisions, support, and rework, not just the initial build. Use a written change-order process when work exceeds scope.

For a buyer, break-even customers = (monthly ad spend + service fee + recurring tools + allocated setup cost) / gross profit per additional customer. Use the buyer's actual profit and close rate; revenue alone overstates what they can afford. Do not describe all attributed sales as incremental sales without evidence.

## Acquisition routine for the next four weeks

Keep this as a bounded experiment. The quantities below are activity targets, not response forecasts.

1. Ask five existing professional contacts for an introduction to a service-business owner who is already investing in growth. Ask permission before naming clients or sharing private results.
2. Each week, research 20 to 25 suitable local businesses. Prioritize a specific observable issue, an active business, a valuable service, and capacity to respond. Do not pay for a scraped SMS list.
3. Prepare five tailored introductions each workday. Mention one verified issue and a relevant example of your work. Offer a short fit conversation. Avoid doing a complete unpaid audit before the owner shows interest. Send only through a channel whose rules and consent requirements are met. This task did not send outreach.
4. Publish two LinkedIn posts per week: one before/after implementation explanation and one practical lesson from real work. Show the problem, what changed, and why it matters. Never imply an unmeasured lift.
5. Build referral relationships with photographers, IT providers, and designers who do not manage Google Ads. Establish scope and referral expectations before exchanging leads.
6. Reply to genuine inquiries within one business day when possible. Ask about service profitability, capacity, response process, and decision authority. Quote after the fit check. Follow up on an outstanding proposal twice, then close the loop politely.

Do not launch paid ads for the portfolio until a specific offer has closed at least a few qualified prospects and its delivery economics are understood. That is a planning recommendation, not a numerical statistical threshold.

## A compact sales pipeline

Use one spreadsheet or CRM with these stages: researched, contacted, replied, qualified, meeting booked, proposal sent, won, lost, onboarding, retained. Record the source, problem, offer, next action/date, proposed price, amount collected, reason lost, delivery hours, and direct costs.

Review weekly: qualified replies/contacted, meetings/qualified replies, proposals/meetings, wins/proposals, cash collected, contribution per owner hour, recurring-client retention, and scope overruns. Read low sample counts as directional evidence. Do not lower price because one prospect declines.

After 10 to 15 qualified proposals, inspect reasons lost. If the offer repeatedly wins and delivery is profitable, test a higher price for new prospects. If prospects see little value, investigate targeting, proof, scope, and messaging before cutting price. Do not change price and targeting simultaneously if you want interpretable results. The goal is collected contribution per hour, not the maximum number of clients.
